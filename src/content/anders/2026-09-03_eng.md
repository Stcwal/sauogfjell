# Mid-semester break post 1

**Disclaimer**: I wrote this on my phone (Norwegian version at least), and had to fight a lot with GitHub. I am sorry if some of the pictures don't work.

After three demanding academic weeks, it was nice to know I had two week of time off ahead. The plan was to bum around by myself for two weeks, but two nice Germans though that was outrageous and asked if I wanted to join them in their van instead. They are from Marburg, known for it's beautiful castle, and Chemnitz, which was European Cultural Capital in 2025. The list of other cities that have been European Cultural Capital contains Kaunas, Lithuania and Novia Sad, Serbia. So this title holds a high star.

They have called their van "Vandalf". I don't really understand what that means, but I think it may be German. *Vandal* is a beautiful Mitsubishi L300. Sadly, it is only insured for two people, so I'm not allowed to try taming the beast.

![Vandalf](./images/2026-09-03_Vandalf.jpg)

Here is a short summary of what the last days have gone to, told via the format compound(?) text


## Monday 2026-08-31

The trip started northwards from Auckland. A litte north of the city, we visited Sheep World. This has been on my list for a long time and I was very happy to finally be able to go. For the first time and activity that truly fits the theme of the blog.

![Sheep World](./images/2026-09-03_Sheepworld.jpg)

For some reason has the German architect Hundertwasser designed a toilet in a tiny New Zealand town called Kawakawa. It was very important for the Germans to visit this, so obviously we did. 

![Hundertwasser](./images/2026-09-03_Hundertwasser.jpg)

We visited the first of what would become many campgrounds. It looked like this when it didn't rain, something that didn't last very long. Big g turned on the faucet quite shortly after this picture, which was not particularly nice.

![Campingplass](./images/2026-09-03_Campingplass.jpg)

## Tuesdag 2026-09-01

We drove to "90 Mile Beach", a veeery long beach. It is in reality only  55 miles, which isn't even 90 kilometer. So here we were served lie after lie. I suspecy some sort of Greenland-sitution, but then they could as well have called it 100 Mile Beach. In my opinion. 90 Mile Beach is classified as a "State Highway", og you can totally drive on it if you have four-wheel drive and it isn't high tide. We had neither, so we had to use our legs instead. The tide has taken many cars, and there are many cool pictures online of cars and buses standing in the water. Presumably not quite as cool to be owner or driver though. 

![Strand](./images/2026-09-03_Strand.jpg)

All the way north at the 90 miles, but not quite, beach, we find "Great Sand Dunes". And they were absolutely great. It was like walking up a small mountain. It felt a bit like walking the mountains in Norway in winter, since it was windy and everything was the same color. None of the pictures we took managed to capture how large these sand dunes actually are, so this picture only looks like it is far away from the beach, and not really high above the sea. 

![Sanddyne](./images/2026-09-03_Sanddyne.jpg)

Finally, we visited New Zealands Lindesnes, i.e. their northernmost point. It is called Cape Rainga og thay have, not that surprising, built a lighthouse here. In the horizon of the picture below, there is allegedly a landmass. The legend says that 1200 years ago, a Maori tribal leader swam to this island. Brave or stupid? Impressive regardless. 

![Fyrtårn](./images/2026-09-03_Fyrtårn.jpg)

## Wednesday 2026-09-02

We hiked to "Duke's Nose". This was allegedly a very tough hike, but it wasn't that bad. Towards the end we had to climb up a small cliff, that was a litt tough maybe. And then the view was really nice from the top. 

![Utsikt](./images/2026-09-03_Utsikt.jpg)

The Germans has started learning some Norwegian, mainly because they repeat what I use to say. At this point they can say "You never know when you need an iron rod", "Good no one got that fat cow in the head", "What about you" and "You little, ugly dwarf". All very important sentences.

## Thursday 2026-09-03

Today we visited "Waitangi Treaty Grounds, the place where the British and the Maori on 6. February 1940 signed an agreement to live together in peace and prosperity, subject to the British Empire of course. The deal existed in two version, one in Maori and one in English. The one in Maori was quite favorable for the Maori, and the one they were presented and subsequently signed. But the British carried the biggest stick og very bendy morals, og enforced instead the one written in English. This one was quite unfavorable for the Maori, and their culture was nearly eradicated by the rules of the Brits. The deal was signed on this lawn:
