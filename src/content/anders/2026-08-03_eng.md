# Journey to New Zealand

The day(s) when I will travel to New Zealand is finally here, and my first blogpost will of course be dedicated to that.

The format is brand new to me, so you'll have to forgive any stylistic mistakes or severe violations of blog-convention.

## Gardermoen

My day started, surprisingly enough at Gardermoen, luckily completely uneventful. In fear of missing the flight (that would have been a colossal blunder), I strolled of the train at 1615, two and a half hours before takeoff. After sneaking through the security control I had two long hours to spend, and I was thus very satisfied with me decision of showing up unecessarily early. 

But it wasn't for nothing, since I got to sole-scrum a little bit, and **substantially** improved the blog. For example, I finally did the mega-fun task of migrating the feedback database from local to remote. That means it is finally possible to send anonymous hate messages by pressing one of the button on the bottom right. I look forward to reading that.

![Smidig utvikling](./images/2026-08-03_Utvikling.jpg)

## Flight Oslo - Istanbul

From the flights in the ordinary plastic planes SAS and Norwegian operate, I have grown use to a quite spartan standard. You sit in a plastic seat, stare at the plastic seat in front of you, get a cup of mediocre tea, and land after no more than two hours. This Turkish Airlines plane had a different standard. The plastic seats remained, but the plastic backside of the seat in front was switched with a screen with games and movies and music. It was delightful to see that they had some Norwegian music in their catalog too, for example Lemaitre's album *Chapter One*, which is very good and definitely recommended. The cup of tea was replaced with a three-course meal consisting of some root vegetables for startes, pasta in a creamed tomato sauce as the main dish and chocolate mousse for dessert. The dinner was good, but I didn't touch the music, despite airplane-quality airplugs. Instead I took the flight as any man without access to a charger and a fear of running out of battery on the phone. Played 2048 and Angry Birds on the screen, and wrote this post.

## Istanbul

Two hours layover. Barely got through the security control (didn't think you needed to do it again, but fine), fill mu bottle and find the gate before boarding. 

## Flight Istanbul - Kuala Lumpur

It is currently 0100 (ish) Norway time, 1100 (ish) Auckland time. Best to go to sleep. Go night to you, blog

When I woke, the man beside me played 2048. Not to diss him or anything, but he was really bad. Swiping left and right and up and down without any plan. That is completely hopeless. I feel like 2048 should be a sort of cheap version of Subway Surfers, but this was simply painful to watch. Instead I beamed up rugby on the screen, and finally got to see the origin of the agile word *scrum*. And I can say here and now that it looks NOTHING like what we learned at the university. I didn't get any wiser when it came to extreme programming, product owner or kanban-board either. It seems like rugby has a way to go when it comes to their pedagogical approach. 

Touchdown after ten hours on the plane, and it was nice knowing I was finally halfway on the journey. 

## Kuala Lumpur

Here I had a four hour layouver, and the gate wouldn't be confirmed until quite late. But when I landed I was quite stressed the gate perhaps was gone, so I *naturally* walked around the terminal and checked that absolutely all the gates were there. And they were. Then the journet is safe.

## Flight Kuala Lumpur - Auckland

Yet another ten hour long flight, but this time the end of the journet waited at the other end. I can reveal that this plane from Malaysia Airlines also was better than the planes of SAS and Norwegian. It was comparable to Turkish Airlines, except that the games were worse. No Angry Birds eller 2048, not even Temple Run for consolation. It wouldn't have been *that* much to ask for.

Here is the view from the plane when approaching Auckland. If you look very closely at the horizon under the wing, you may see som tall buildings/towers. They are in the city centre, which is approximately where I live. The area in the foreground I guess is an Auckland version of Lillestrøm or Jessheim.

![Auckland fra flyet](./images/2026-08-03_Auckland.jpg)

## Auckland

Now I have officially sat a new record of being the furthest away from home. When I arrived there were 9 fresh degrees and sunshine. Managed to buy both a mobile subscription and adapter to the phone charger, so now I can watch reels while walking to the university without worry. It also means I can't be reached via SMS or direct calls. Try Whatsapp or Signal or Messenger. Or Snapchat if it is an emergency. 

Current time is 0646 Norway-time. It is well over 26 hours since I left home. I have found my new home in my new home at Moholt Studentby, or Te Tirohanda, which they have chosen to call it here. I am tired. I will blog more later.
