# My first battle with the bureaucracy

Yesterday I saw a movie from New Zealand called *Hunt for the Wilderpeople*, directed by Taika Waititi. It is a about a young boy who has gone from foster home to foster home and is eventually placed at a lifestyle block in the New Zealand wilderness. I liked the movie very much; it was both funny and exciting, so it is highly recommended. In this movie we also met a character that had moved away from society and into the woods. He said that the government forced you to fill out way too many forms, and he was sick of it. And if you want to stop filling out forms? Then you have to fill out like four new forms just to be allowed to do that. Haha, so funny with the crazy man that says the government forces you to fill out all those forms.

![Hunt for the Wilderpeople](./images/2026-08-10_Wilderpeople.jpg)

The only valid ID we as Norwegian citizens have in New Zealand is a Norwegian passport, which means we have to carry our passport around whenever we want to get into a nightclub or buy beer at the store. If you don't want to risk losing your passport you can either acquire a [Kiwi Access Card](https://kiwiaccess.co.nz/) or a New Zealand driver's license, which both are valid ID. The Norwegian driver's license is not accepted. How the Legoland-Id on the left is good enough, but not the EEA-license on the left doesn't cut it is a mystery to me, but that's how it is down here.

![Førerkort](./images/2026-08-10_Førerkort.png)

Today I went to my local New Zealand Automobile Association-office to convert the Norwegian license to a New Zealand license. Since Norway is on a sortof nice-list, I don't need a driver's test or amything like that, which is quite convenient. It costs a few dollars, but I feel it is worth it. Therefore I show up hopeful at the office with my passport, Norwegian license and international license, which is a translation of my driver's license valid for driving in New Zealand issued by a government approved body in Norway. 

At the traffic station the first thing I meet is a wall of forms, for every thinkable case you can come to the traffic station with. ...just like the man said.

![Skjemaer](./images/2026-08-10_Skjemaer.jpg)

Oh well, guess I'll just fill out that form then, so I can get the New Zealand driver's license. Fill out the form. Show up at the desk. The international drivers license is not a valid translation of your drivers license. But it is a valid license in New Zealand. Yes, but it is not a valid translation. But it is in English. It is the wrong English. You need a translation from a translator approved by the *government*. Okay. You can find one at this website. Og then you must fill out their form. And then you must wait for an answer. And then you must print out the translation and bring to us. Then we will consider your case. 

I do currently *not* have a New Zealand driver's license, and must still use the passport as id.
